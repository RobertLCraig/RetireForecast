# Ask the managing agent four questions

## What I need from you

**One email to the managing agent, asking for four things.**

1. The last three years' service charge accounts.
2. The reserve fund balance.
3. Any five or ten year planned maintenance programme.
4. Any section 20 notices of intention served, or expected.

While you have them, ask whether the block is a higher-risk building under the building safety
rules, and whether the leaseholders' contribution cap applies.

---

**Why.** The model escalates service charges smoothly and has **no lumpy capital item anywhere**. A
section 20 major-works demand on a block of this age and construction is legally enforceable,
cannot be deferred, and arrives as one bill. On the tool's own figures the survivor's free-to-choose
money is a very small amount per month, so a single major-works demand is many years of that margin
arriving in one letter.

The property reviewer's working assumption, absent real numbers, is two significant demands across
the plan - one in the 2030s and one in the 2040s. He would not sign off any keep-the-flat plan
without seeing the actual programme.

The accounts matter for a second reason. The escalator itself is probably too benign. Block
insurance, building-safety compliance and energy costs inside a service charge have all compounded
faster than general inflation since 2019. Three years of real accounts settle whether that is true
of this block, and the difference over a long plan is thousands a year of real spend in the survivor
years. It is one of the few single inputs that could flip which plan wins.

The building safety question is worth ten minutes for two reasons: a qualifying leaseholder's
contribution is capped, and an unresolved building-safety position is one of the standard reasons a
block's flats sell slowly - which is exactly what the declining surveyor described.

**Pass** is the accounts, the reserve balance and a written answer on planned works.

**Fail** is a verbal "nothing planned". Ask for the programme document.

**Why it needs you.** Only the leaseholder can ask. Cards 0028 and 0029 are both waiting on real
numbers rather than assumed ones.
