# Get the winning plan's mortgage actually quoted

## What I need from you

**One question to the broker.** On a purchase of the size in the sell-and-buy-cheaper plan, with
the household's income after the working partner retires, what interest-only or retirement-interest
-only rate could they actually get, and would it pass the survivor affordability test?

---

**Why this is not a detail.** Every keep-the-flat route in this project has been judged against
real, dated broker evidence - and killed by it. Seven intermediaries over nineteen months, each
producing a smaller maximum advance than the last, and the retirement-interest-only route died on
survivor affordability in August.

The plan that currently wins is judged against a rate **typed into a form**. The base scenario
carries a buy-side mortgage rate that nobody has quoted, and the purchase is funded partly by
borrowing at that rate for life.

That asymmetry of evidence is doing real work in the ranking. The two leading plans are less than a
point apart, which the board itself calls noise, and one of them has been tested against the market
while the other has not.

There is a second reason to ask. The property reviewer expects it to pass - the borrowing is small
relative to the survivor's income - but "should pass" is not a quote, and it is the same test that
killed the alternative.

**Pass** is a written indication naming a rate and confirming the survivor affordability position.

**Fail** is a verbal "that should be fine".

**Why it needs you.** Only you can put the question to the broker. Card 0034 is a related engine
bug - the purchase currently cannot see money arriving the same year, so it borrows when it need
not - and fixing that may remove the need for the borrowing altogether. Worth mentioning to the
broker that the amount may fall.
