# Lasting powers of attorney, before anyone signs a mortgage deed

## What I need from you
The recheck date (16 September) has passed. Tell me where each of these stands:
1. Lasting powers of attorney (LPAs: legal permission for someone to act for you), two each for both partners: property and financial affairs, and health and welfare. Four in all. Pass: all four registered with the Office of the Public Guardian, confirmations received.
2. The Land Registry title register for the flat. Pass: you know whether they own it as joint tenants or tenants in common.
3. Wills. Pass: both confirmed current, or a solicitor booked.

**My recommendation:** do these before anything else on the board. Never sign a mortgage or equity-release deed before the LPAs are registered.
Paste to answer: `**2026-10-07** **Decided:** LPAs: <registered / applied on date / not started>. Title: <joint tenants / tenants in common>. Wills: <current / solicitor booked>.`

## What you need to know
- Every plan needs someone to sign a mortgage or a sale.
- If the older partner loses capacity before a property and financial affairs LPA is registered, the other partner cannot remortgage, sell, or sign an equity-release deed on the jointly owned flat.
- The only way out then is a Court of Protection deputyship: months of delay and a supervision fee for life. A lender will not accept a general power of attorney.
- Cost: a registration fee per LPA (reduced or waived on low income), plus a small Land Registry fee.
- Worth an hour with a solicitor: if they are joint tenants, the older partner's half passes to the survivor and then counts in her care means test. Splitting the ownership and leaving that half in a life-interest trust lets her live there for life while keeping it out of the test. The estate planner called it the highest-value step available. It interacts with any equity-release deed, so settle the wills first.
