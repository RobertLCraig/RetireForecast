# Lasting powers of attorney, before anyone signs a mortgage deed

## What I need from you

**Three things, in this order.**

1. Register a lasting power of attorney for property and financial affairs, and one for health and
   welfare, for **both** partners. Four in total.
2. Order the Land Registry title register for the flat and tell me how the two of them own it -
   joint tenants or tenants in common.
3. Confirm whether each of them has a current will, and book a solicitor if not.

---

**Why this is first.** Every plan on the board needs somebody to sign a mortgage or a sale. If the
older partner loses capacity before a property and financial affairs power is **registered**, the
other partner cannot remortgage, sell, or execute an equity-release deed on a jointly owned
property. The only route left is a Court of Protection deputyship: months of delay and a
supervision fee for life. A lender will not accept a general power - it must be a registered
lasting power.

The tool's own care model gives the older partner a real probability of a period where capacity is
doubtful. This is the cheapest item in the whole expert review and it is the one that voids
everything else.

**Pass** is all four of:
- both powers registered for both partners, confirmations received
- the title register downloaded and the form of co-ownership known
- both wills confirmed current, or a solicitor appointment booked
- none of this waiting on the outcome of card 0022

**Fail** is signing any mortgage or equity-release deed before the powers are registered. If that
is already in motion, say so here and I will flag it on the affected scenarios.

**Why it needs you.** These are signatures and a fee, not a modelling question. Cost is the
registration fee per power, with remission available on low income, plus a small charge for the
title register. Nothing in the repo can settle whether the wills exist.

**One more thing worth an hour of a solicitor's time.** If they own the flat as joint tenants, the
older partner's half passes automatically to the survivor and is then fully exposed to her care
means test for the rest of her life. Severing the tenancy and leaving that half on a life-interest
trust would let her live there for life while keeping that half out of her assessment. The estate
planner called it the single highest-value estate-planning act available here. It is **not**
deprivation of assets - it disposes of an asset that was never hers. But it interacts with any
equity-release deed, which is another reason to settle the wills first.
